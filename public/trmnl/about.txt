    <strong>A window into the abyss.</strong><br>
    Turn your TRMNL into your personal window into the deep ocean. Abyss lets you peek into a fascinating corner of our planet: the deep Pacific. Look into hydrothermal vent habitats on Axial Seamount, an active underwater volcano, a methane seep at Southern Hydrate Ridge, and the Oregon Offshore seafloor. These are real research camera images from places most of us will never visit.<br><br>
    <strong>Choose your view</strong><br>
    Abyss selects stills from these cameras&#x27; periodic recordings and snapshots. Cycle through all four cameras, choose a few favourites, or settle into one. The default 15-minute refresh rotates your selected views. Images are processed every three hours, and a camera&#x27;s last good image stays on display when it cannot update.<br><br>
    <strong>Where are the cameras?</strong><br>
    Four camera views, three research sites in the northeast Pacific. Both vent cameras are on Axial Seamount; the methane-seep camera is at Southern Hydrate Ridge, and the seafloor camera is at Oregon Offshore. Depths are approximate.<br><br>
    <strong>ASHES: Mushroom vent</strong><br>
    <strong>about 1,540 m below the surface.</strong> On Axial Seamount, an active submarine volcano on the Juan de Fuca Ridge, hundreds of kilometres off the Oregon coast. This camera looks into the ASHES hydrothermal vent field, where hot fluids emerge from the volcanic seafloor.<br><br>
    <a href="https://interactiveoceans.washington.edu/research-sites/axial-caldera/ashes/">Explore the research site</a> &middot; <a href="https://s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/media.ooica.net/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/06193730/ASHES-Map-2020.png">Detailed site map</a><br><br>
    <strong>International District: Tiny Towers</strong><br>
    <strong>about 1,520 m below the surface.</strong> A second vent field on the same volcano, Axial Seamount. The Tiny Towers camera gives us another view of its hydrothermal habitat. International District is the name of this seafloor vent field.<br><br>
    <a href="https://interactiveoceans.washington.edu/research-sites/axial-caldera/international-district/">Explore the research site</a> &middot; <a href="https://s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/media.ooica.net/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/06193821/Tiny-Towers-Hydrothermal-Field-Map-2020.png">Detailed site map</a><br><br>
    <strong>Southern Hydrate Ridge: Einstein&#x27;s Grotto</strong><br>
    <strong>about 780 m below the surface.</strong> About 90 km west of Newport, Oregon, on the Cascadia continental margin. Here the camera looks across Einstein&#x27;s Grotto, an active methane seep at Southern Hydrate Ridge. This is a separate site from the volcanic vent fields at Axial Seamount.<br><br>
    <a href="https://interactiveoceans.washington.edu/research-sites/southern-hydrate-ridge/">Explore the research site</a> &middot; <a href="https://s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/media.ooica.net/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/28232704/Southern-Hydrate-Map.png">Detailed site map</a><br><br>
    <strong>Oregon Offshore: seafloor</strong><br>
    <strong>about 580 m below the surface.</strong> On the continental slope off Oregon, the CAMDSB106 camera looks across the seabed from the Oregon Offshore benthic platform. Fish and small seabed animals appear in its periodic snapshots.<br><br>
    <a href="https://interactiveoceans.washington.edu/research-sites/oregon-offshore/">Explore the research site</a><br><br>

    <strong>Made possible by open ocean science</strong><br>
    This window is made possible by public investment in science. The U.S. National Science Foundation funds the Ocean Observatories Initiative, whose Regional Cabled Array connects deep-ocean instruments to shore through a 900-kilometre network of power and data cables. Scientists, engineers and research crews keep this observatory running, and open data lets the rest of us look in, right from your TRMNL.<br><br>
